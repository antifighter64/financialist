The Business Model: Contingency Appeals

Property tax appeal services work on contingency: they review your assessment, file an appeal with your county, and take a percentage of whatever they save you. You pay nothing upfront.

Typical contingency fees run 25–50% of first-year savings (industry standard — check any provider's terms). So:

  • Your home is assessed at $500,000; appeal wins a reduction to $450,000
  • At a 1.5% tax rate, that's $750/year in savings
  • The service takes 35%: $262.50
  • You keep: $487.50/year, every year until reassessment

The service does this at scale — software pulls comparable sales, auto-generates appeal filings, and handles hearings. Marginal cost per appeal is low. At $262 revenue per win, the math works even with modest success rates.

Now the $15 makes sense. If 1 in 5 signups becomes a paying appeal customer (we estimate), the acquisition cost is $75 per customer — against $262+ in first-year revenue. Profitable on day one, before multi-year savings.

Why Assessments Are Wrong (Often)

This isn't a conspiracy — it's structural:

  1. Mass appraisal. Counties assess thousands of properties using models, not inspections. Models lag the market and miss property-specific issues (that cracked foundation, the highway noise).
  2. Asymmetric appeals. Wealthy homeowners and commercial properties appeal routinely; everyone else doesn't. Assessors know this. The system effectively over-taxes people who don't appeal.
  3. Lagging data. Assessments often trail market downturns by 1–2 years. If your market dipped and your assessment didn't, you're overpaying.

Industry studies have consistently found that a meaningful share of residential assessments exceed market value — and that appeals succeed at high rates when filed with comparable-sales evidence. The gold is real.

Who This Is For

Strong candidates:

  • Homeowners in areas with recent market declines
  • Anyone whose assessment jumped sharply year-over-year
  • Owners of unique properties (models handle these worst)
  • Landlords with multiple properties (scale the savings)

Weak candidates:

  • Recent buyers (your purchase price just set the market value)
  • Hot markets where assessments lag below market (appealing could backfire — though reputable services won't file losing appeals)
  • States with assessment caps (California's Prop 13, for example, already limits increases)

DIY vs. Service: The Real Comparison

You can appeal yourself — it's free. Here's the honest tradeoff:

DIY appeal:

  • Cost: $0 + 3–6 hours of your time
  • You keep 100% of savings
  • Requires: pulling comps, filling county forms, possibly attending a hearing
  • Success rate: decent if you do the homework (comparable sales are public record)

Using a service (like Ownwell):

  • Cost: 25–50% of first-year savings
  • You keep 50–75%, zero time invested
  • They handle: comps, filing, hearings, follow-up
  • Success rate: higher (they do this thousands of times)

The breakeven math: if your time is worth $50/hour and DIY takes 5 hours, that's $250 in time cost. On a $750/year savings, the service takes ~$262 and you net $487 with zero hours. DIY nets you $750 minus $250 in time = $500 effective. It's close — the service wins on convenience, DIY wins if your time is cheap or you enjoy the process.

The $15 Lead Funnel

What happens after you sign up:

  1. Property verification. You confirm your address and property details. The affiliate earns $15.
  2. Assessment analysis. Their software pulls your assessment, comparable sales, and estimates appeal odds — usually within days.
  3. The pitch. If the numbers work, they show projected savings and ask you to authorize the appeal (contingency agreement).
  4. Filing and hearing. They file, present evidence, negotiate with the assessor.
  5. Savings and fee. If they win, you get the reduction; they bill their percentage.

If the analysis shows your assessment is fair, reputable services tell you — filing a doomed appeal wastes everyone's time. (This is also why the payout is per verified signup, not per appeal: the company wants real properties in the funnel, and they'll filter from there.)

Red Flags in the Industry

The contingency model is legitimate, but watch for:

  • Upfront fees. Reputable appeal services charge contingency only. Anyone asking for money before results is suspect.
  • Savings guarantees. No one can guarantee an appeal outcome. Guarantees are marketing.
  • Multi-year fee lock-in. Some contracts claim a percentage of savings for 2–3 years. Read the agreement — one year is standard.
  • Filing without authorization. Never sign a blank authorization. You should approve each appeal.

Why Affiliates Love This Niche

$15/signup with a 45-day cookie, in a niche with:

  • Genuine consumer need (everyone overpays property tax)
  • Low content competition (few finance sites cover it well)

  • Recurring relevance (assessments happen every year)

It's the kind of niche NerdWallet hasn't fully colonized — which is exactly why it's here and not there.

Bottom Line

$15 per signup. Contingency fees. A system that structurally over-assesses the people least likely to appeal.

Check your assessment. If it looks high, either appeal it yourself or let a service take their cut. Either way, the money you're leaving on the table is yours — go get it.
