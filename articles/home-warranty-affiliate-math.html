Why $20? Follow the Premiums

A typical home warranty plan costs $350–$600/year. The average homeowner keeps it for 2–3 years (we estimate, based on industry retention patterns). Lifetime revenue per customer: roughly $700–$1,800.

But here's the key: home warranty loss ratios are low. Unlike health or auto insurance, where 60–80% of premiums go to claims, home warranties are service contracts with heavy exclusions. Industry analysts have long noted that home warranty companies pay out far less in claims than traditional insurers do relative to premiums collected — that's the entire business model.

So the math for the warranty company:

  • Pay $20 for a quote lead
  • We estimate 5–10% of quote requests become customers → $200–$400 acquisition cost per customer
  • Customer pays $450/year × 2.5 years = ~$1,125 lifetime revenue
  • Claims + service costs well under half of that (we estimate)

The $20 lead is cheap for what it buys. That's why the quote forms are everywhere.

The Session Cookie: A Tell

Most affiliate programs give 30–90 day cookies. Choice Home Warranty uses a session-based referral — the cookie lasts only for your browsing session.

Why? Because home warranty is an impulse-adjacent purchase. You search "home warranty quote" when your AC dies or you're closing on a house — moments of urgency. The company knows you won't comparison-shop for three weeks. You'll decide today.

The session cookie tells you the industry's own view of its sales cycle: fast, urgent, emotional. That's useful information for you as a buyer. It means: slow down. The urgency is manufactured by your broken appliance, not by the market.

What "Correct Consumer Info" Means

The payout triggers on an "instant quote (page completed with correct consumer info)." That parenthetical is doing heavy lifting.

It means the affiliate only gets paid when you submit real, verifiable information — name, address, phone, email, property details. Fake data = no payout. This is fraud prevention: the industry has been burned by bot-submitted quote forms.

What it means for you: the form isn't casual. You're handing over enough personal data to be a verified sales lead. Expect:

  1. An instant quote (the number on screen)
  2. A phone call within hours — home warranty sales teams are aggressive; same-day calls are standard
  3. Follow-up emails for weeks — "your quote is expiring" (it isn't, really)
  4. Your data in their CRM permanently

The Home Warranty Value Question

Let's be honest about the product, because the affiliate economics only make sense if you understand what you're buying.

What a home warranty covers: repair/replacement of major systems (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) and appliances, up to annual caps (typically $1,500–$3,000 per item).

What it doesn't cover: pre-existing conditions, improper maintenance, code violations, and — critically — anything the contract's fine print excludes. Denied claims are the #1 consumer complaint about home warranties, consistently ranking high in state attorney general complaint data.

The service fee: every claim costs you $60–$125 out of pocket, on top of the annual premium.

The math for you:

Cost
Annual premium$450
2 claims × $75 service fee$150
Total year 1$600

If nothing breaks: you're out $600. If your $800 dishwasher dies and they cover it minus the $75 fee: you "saved" $125 but spent $600 to do it. The warranty only wins if a major system fails — and then you're fighting the exclusions.

Who it makes sense for: first-time buyers of older homes who can't absorb a $5,000 HVAC replacement. It's peace-of-mind insurance, not an investment.

Who should skip it: anyone with an emergency fund, new construction (builder warranties cover you), or the discipline to self-insure.

How to Shop If You Want One

Get 3 quotes. Prices and coverage vary enormously. The $20 affiliate lead means every site wants your form — use that leverage.

Read the sample contract before buying. Not the marketing page — the actual contract. The exclusions section is the real product description.

Check your state's complaint data. State insurance commissioners (home warranties are regulated as service contracts in most states) publish complaint ratios. A company with 3x the complaints of competitors is telling you something.

Negotiate. The first quote is never the best price. Mention a competitor's quote. Ask for the service fee to be waived on the first claim. These companies negotiate — the $20 they paid for your lead means they need the sale.

Never buy under pressure. "This quote expires today" is a sales tactic. The quote doesn't expire. Your AC being broken doesn't obligate you to buy from the first company that calls.

Why This Content Exists

Home warranty affiliate content exists because $20/lead × high search volume ("home warranty" gets massive searches from new homeowners) = profitable publishing. The content isn't necessarily bad — but understand the incentive: the site wants your form submission, and the warranty company wants your $450/year.

Nobody in the chain is incentivized to tell you that self-insuring with an emergency fund usually wins. We just did — for free.

Bottom Line

$20 per quote. Session cookie. Aggressive follow-up. That's the home warranty lead economy.

If you need the peace of mind, shop hard and read the contract. If you have savings, skip it — $600/year in an emergency fund beats $600/year in premiums with exclusions.

Either way, now you know what your quote form is worth.
