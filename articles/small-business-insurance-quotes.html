The Small Business Insurance Quote Game: What $30 Per Quote Really Buys

If you run a small business, your insurance quote request is worth $30. Not to you — to the website that sent you.

By Financialist · October 17, 2026

If you run a small business, your insurance quote request is worth $30. Not to you — to the website that sent you.

Small business insurance is one of the least understood, most lucrative corners of the affiliate economy. Here's what the $30 buys, why quotes are the currency, and how to shop without getting played.

The Numbers

ProgramPayoutTrigger
Simply Business US$30Per quoted RFQ (request for quote)
Hiscox Small Business Insurance$15Per quote delivered to customer

$30 for a quote request. $15 for a delivered quote. These are among the highest per-action payouts in insurance affiliate marketing — higher than Aflac's $16 health quote, higher than most auto insurance leads.

Why Quotes Are the Currency

Most insurance sells on price comparison. Small business insurance doesn't — because every business is different.

A freelance designer needs professional liability (errors & omissions). A food truck needs general liability + commercial auto + workers' comp. A consultant needs cyber liability. There is no "standard" small business policy, which means:

  1. No instant pricing. The insurer needs to know your revenue, headcount, industry, claims history, and coverage limits before quoting. This takes a human or a sophisticated form.
  2. The quote IS the product. By the time you've completed a 10-minute RFQ form, you're a qualified, high-intent buyer. The insurer would rather pay $30 for you than $5 for ten tire-kickers.
  3. Policies renew annually. A $30 lead that becomes a $1,200/year policy renewing for 5+ years is worth $6,000+ in premiums. The math is as lopsided as life insurance.

What "Quoted RFQ" Actually Means

Simply Business pays $30 per quoted RFQ — not per form submission, but per request that actually receives a quote.

This is a quality filter. If you submit a half-completed form, or your business doesn't fit their underwriting appetite, no quote goes out — and the affiliate earns nothing. The $30 only triggers when the system (or an agent) prices your risk and delivers a number.

Why this matters to you: the form is designed to extract exactly the underwriting data needed. Every question — revenue band, employee count, years in business, prior claims — feeds the pricing engine. Answer accurately; inaccurate answers get you a quote that won't survive underwriting, wasting everyone's time.

The Hiscox $15: Quote Delivered

Hiscox pays $15 when the customer receives a quote. Lower than Simply Business's $30, but the trigger is simpler — delivery, not a full RFQ process.

The $15–$30 range tells you the market rate for a qualified small business insurance shopper: roughly the cost of a nice lunch to acquire a customer worth thousands.

The Broker vs. Direct Split

Here's what the affiliate payouts reveal about the industry structure:

Simply Business is a broker — they shop multiple carriers and take a commission (typically 10–15% of premium). Their $30 affiliate payout is funded by that commission spread across thousands of policies.

Hiscox is a direct insurer — they underwrite their own policies. Their $15 payout comes straight from their customer acquisition budget.

As a buyer, the broker model (Simply Business) gets you comparison across carriers in one form. The direct model (Hiscox) gets you one carrier's price, usually faster. Neither is inherently better — but now you know who's paying for your click.

What Happens After You Request a Quote

  1. Instant quotes (simple risks): you get a number in minutes. The affiliate earns their $15–$30.
  2. Referred quotes (complex risks): an agent calls within 1–2 business days. Your RFQ data is now in their CRM.
  3. The follow-up sequence: expect 3–5 touches (call, email, call) over two weeks. Small business insurance has a long consideration cycle, and agents are persistent.
  4. Renewal marketing: even if you don't buy, you're now a known insurance shopper. Expect annual "time to renew?" outreach.

How to Shop Smart

Know your coverage before you quote. The three policies most small businesses need:

CoverageWhat it doesWho needs it
General liabilityBodily injury / property damage claimsEveryone with customers or a premises
Professional liability (E&O)Claims of negligence / mistakesService businesses, consultants, agencies
Workers' compensationEmployee injury (legally required in most states)Anyone with employees

Get 3 quotes minimum. Prices for the same coverage can vary 2–3x between carriers for small businesses, because underwriting models differ wildly by industry niche.

Don't over-insure. Agents earn commission on premium size. A solo consultant doesn't need a $2M umbrella policy. Match coverage to actual risk.

Read the exclusions. The cheapest quote usually has the most exclusions. Professional liability that excludes your actual profession's core risk is worthless.

Ask about the claims process before you buy. The true test of business insurance is the claim. Ask: who handles claims, what's the average resolution time, is there a dedicated rep?

The Bigger Picture

Small business insurance is a $100+ billion market in the US, and most of it is still sold through agents and brokers — not direct-to-consumer. The $30 affiliate quote is the industry's attempt to digitize the top of the funnel.

For publishers, it's a sweet spot: high payout ($30), underserved content (few sites cover it well), and genuine reader need (most small business owners are underinsured).

For you, the buyer: your quote request is valuable. Act like it. Get multiple quotes, take your time, and never let an agent rush you into a policy.

Bottom Line

$30 per quote. $15 per delivered quote. Your small business insurance shopping is worth real money to real companies — because a $1,200/year policy renewing for a decade is worth $12,000.

Shop like someone worth $12,000. Because you are.
