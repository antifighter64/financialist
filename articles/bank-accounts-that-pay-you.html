7 Bank Accounts That Pay You Up to $300 Just for Applying

Most people open a bank account and get nothing. A free toaster, if they're lucky.

By Financialist · October 17, 2026

Most people open a bank account and get nothing. A free toaster, if they're lucky.

But banks pay affiliates serious money for every application. We know, because we checked. Some banks pay up to $300 for a single submitted application — before you deposit a dollar.

Here's the part that matters to you: banks offering big acquisition payouts usually pair them with big signup bonuses for you. Below are the 7 accounts worth your application in 2026, ranked by what you get.

1. Thrivent Bank — Best Overall Payout

Thrivent Bank pays $300 per submitted account-open application. That's the highest per-application payout we've seen from any bank affiliate program. When a bank is willing to pay that much for an application, it means they're competing hard for customers — and competing banks offer better terms.

Why apply: Thrivent is a well-established financial institution with a strong reputation in the values-based banking space. High acquisition spending usually means aggressive signup bonuses and competitive rates.

Watch out for: As with any bank, read the account terms. Minimum balance requirements and fee schedules vary by account type.

2. BMO — Best for Homebuyers ($225 per Mortgage Application)

BMO pays $225 for every completed mortgage application and $175 for every completed HELOC application. You don't have to close the loan. You just have to apply.

Why apply: If you're shopping for a mortgage or a home equity line of credit, you should be comparing multiple lenders anyway. BMO is one of North America's largest banks, and applying costs you nothing but 20 minutes.

The smart move: Apply to 3–4 lenders when rate shopping. Each application is a data point. BMO's willingness to pay $225 per application tells you they're hungry for mortgage business — hungry lenders negotiate.

3. Experian Smart Money — Best Free Checking + Credit Monitoring

Experian pays up to $50 per Smart Money checking account opened. But the real value here is the bundle: a checking account from the same company that runs your credit reports, paired with free credit monitoring.

Why apply: If you're already checking your Experian credit report, keeping your checking account in the same ecosystem is convenient. No monthly fee.

Watch out for: Experian makes money by selling you upgrades. The free tier is genuinely useful — just don't get upsold on things you don't need.

4. Zacks Trade — Best for Active Traders ($70 per Account Opened)

Zacks Trade pays $70 for every new brokerage account opened. No funding requirement for the payout — just open the account.

How to Play This Right

Stack them. There's no rule against opening multiple accounts. Open the checking account, the brokerage account, and set up direct deposit — they're different products from different companies.

Never pay to apply. Every account on this list is free to open. If any bank asks you for money upfront to "process" your application, walk away.

Read the fee schedule. The signup bonus is a one-time event. The monthly fee is forever. A $300 bonus means nothing if you're paying $15/month in fees for three years.

Keep your emergency fund separate. Chasing bonuses is fun. But your emergency fund belongs in a boring, reliable, high-yield savings account you never touch.

The Bottom Line

Banks will pay up to $300 just for your application. That tells you everything about how much your business is worth to them. Act like it — compare offers, negotiate, and never settle for the first account a bank puts in front of you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to deposit money to get the bonus?

It depends on the offer. Some bonuses require a minimum deposit or direct deposit within 90 days. Others pay on application alone. Always read the offer terms before applying — the requirements are spelled out, usually in fine print at the bottom of the signup page.

Will applying hurt my credit score?

Checking and savings account applications usually trigger only a soft pull (or a ChexSystems check), which doesn't affect your credit score. Brokerage accounts also typically use soft pulls. Mortgage applications are different — they involve a hard inquiry. But rate shopping for a mortgage within a 14–45 day window counts as a single inquiry on most scoring models, so apply to several lenders at once.

Can I close the account after getting the bonus?

Usually yes, but check for early-closure fees. Some banks charge $25–$50 if you close within 90–180 days. The smart play: keep the account open at least 6 months, then decide whether it's worth keeping long-term.

Are these bonuses taxable?

Yes. Bank signup bonuses are considered interest income by the IRS. If your bonus is $600 or more, the bank will send you a 1099-INT. Even below $600, you're technically supposed to report it. Keep records.

Why do banks pay so much for applications?

Customer acquisition cost. The average bank spends $200–$400 to acquire a new checking customer through advertising. Paying $300 per application to an affiliate is actually cheaper than their other marketing channels — and the lifetime value of a banking customer is worth thousands.
