Best personal loans: the fee hiding behind the monthly payment

SoFi, Upgrade and LendingTree give borrowers three different ways to shop. The lowest payment is not automatically the lowest cost, especially when a fee is removed before the money reaches you.

By James Park · Checked September 27, 2026
How we chose: We compared public product terms, eligibility, fee disclosures and regulator guidance. These are curated editorial shortlists, not numerical ratings or claims that we tested the services. Get a current, personal offer before signing.

A $10,000 loan can mean less than $10,000 in hand

Upgrade's own example shows a $10,000 loan with a 5% origination charge sending $9,500 to the borrower. The full $10,000 principal still affects repayment. If your goal is to pay an exact existing balance, check the disbursed cash and the amount that must be borrowed to meet it. Upgrade currently lists an APR range of 7.74%-35.99%, but the individual rate depends on underwriting and discounts; some offers are not available in every state.

SoFi's published personal-loan examples include no-origination-fee terms. A direct-pay discount requires eligible creditors and specified use of proceeds; it is not a universal discount. Its individual offer can differ from example terms. LendingTree is a comparison marketplace, not a lender or a guarantee that a borrower will receive five offers.

How to compare a real offer

The CFPB says personal installment loans can have origination, documentation, late, and other fees. Compare APR on the same loan amount and term, but also compare cash disbursed, total payments, payment timing and any late or secured-loan fees. Prequalification estimates can change after a lender verifies the application.

If borrowing to pay credit cards, run the alternative through Financialist's debt payoff calculator and keep the card balances from rebuilding. If your accounts are already delinquent, a loan may not fit at all. Our debt relief comparison explains that different risk.

Questions before signing

  • How much cash will actually arrive after any withheld fee?
  • Is the rate fixed, and which autopay or direct-pay conditions matter?
  • What is the total repaid at the stated term, not just the monthly payment?
  • Does the lender require a car or other collateral?
  • Will a full application trigger a hard credit inquiry?
