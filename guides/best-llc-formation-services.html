1. ZenBusiness - 4.7/5 - Best overall

ZenBusiness hits the balance this category usually misses: a $0 starter tier that is actually usable, an interface built for first-time founders, and ongoing-compliance tools (annual reports, amendments) that matter more than the formation itself over a company's life. Upsells exist - they exist everywhere in this industry - but they are clearly priced and easy to decline.

2. Bizee - 4.5/5 - Best free formation

Bizee (formerly Incfile) files your LLC for $0 plus the state fee and includes a year of registered agent service free - the single best sticker price in the category. The tradeoffs are a thinner dashboard and more email upsell pressure than ZenBusiness. For a founder who knows exactly what they need, it is the value pick.

Scorecard: Pricing 4.9/5 | Ease of use 4.3/5 | Compliance tools 4.2/5 | Support 4.2/5

Head-to-head: ZenBusiness vs Bizee

Both file for $0 plus state fees. ZenBusiness wins on product - dashboard, compliance tooling, first-timer guidance - and is the better default for most people. Bizee wins on pure cost including the free registered-agent year, and suits founders who will manage compliance themselves. After year one, compare registered-agent renewal prices on both before deciding - that is where the real lifetime cost lives.

What actually matters when forming an LLC

  • State fees are fixed by the state, not the service - anyone charging more "for the state" is padding.
  • Registered agent is mandatory. A free first year is standard; renewal pricing ($99-$199/yr) is the number to compare.
  • You need an EIN (free from the IRS in minutes - never pay for one alone), an operating agreement (most services include a template), and annual report filings in your state.
  • Form in the state where you do business. The Delaware/Wyoming pitch helps specific situations (outside investors, holding companies), not most small businesses - forming elsewhere means registering in your home state too and paying twice.

FAQ

Is $0 formation really free? The service fee is $0; the state filing fee is not. Expect $50-$500 depending on the state.

How long does it take? A few days to a few weeks by state; rush options exist in most states for a state-set fee.

LLC or sole proprietorship? The LLC's liability shield costs little and separates business and personal assets - for anything beyond a hobby, it is the default answer.
