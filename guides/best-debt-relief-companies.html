1. National Debt Relief - 4.8/5 - Best overall

National Debt Relief is the largest dedicated settlement company in the country and the default first call for most people carrying $10,000 or more in unsecured debt. The fee structure is the industry standard - 15-25% of enrolled debt, paid only as settlements are reached - and the company's scale means it negotiates with every major card issuer daily.

Scorecard: Fees 4.5/5 | Track record 5/5 | Customer experience 4.8/5 | Availability 4.9/5

Pros: largest negotiating bench; no upfront fees; strong settlement documentation.

Cons: $7,500 minimum; settlement hurts credit in the short term - true of every company on this list.

2. Freedom Debt Relief - 4.6/5 - Best for large debts

Freedom Debt Relief has run settlement programs since 2002 and is the category's other giant. For straightforward credit-card and personal-loan balances above $15,000, its creditor coverage and two decades of documented outcomes make it the safest pair of hands at scale.

Scorecard: Fees 4.5/5 | Track record 4.9/5 | Customer experience 4.5/5 | Availability 4.7/5

Pros: deepest creditor coverage; long operating history; most states covered.

Cons: no tax-debt lane; minimum $7,500.

3. CuraDebt - 4.4/5 - Best for smaller debts and tax debt

CuraDebt is the boutique on this list, operating since 2001, and it earns its place twice: its $5,000 minimum serves balances the giants turn away, and it runs a dedicated IRS and state tax-debt line that neither National Debt Relief nor Freedom touches.

Scorecard: Fees 4.3/5 | Track record 4.4/5 | Customer experience 4.6/5 | Availability 3.9/5

Pros: lowest minimum on the list; tax-debt capability; hands-on service.

Cons: narrower state footprint; smaller negotiating bench.

Head-to-head: National Debt Relief vs Freedom Debt Relief

Both charge 15-25% of enrolled debt, both start at $7,500, and both run 24-48 month programs. The difference is fit, not price. National Debt Relief edges the comparison on customer experience and program management - it is our pick for most readers. Freedom pulls ahead for very large, multi-creditor balances where its negotiating bench matters most. If your debt includes anything owed to the IRS or a state, skip both and call CuraDebt.

What to know before you enroll

  • Settlement programs ask you to stop paying creditors while a settlement fund builds. Your credit score will drop before it recovers.
  • Forgiven debt over $600 can be taxable as income - ask each company how they handle 1099-C reporting.
  • No legitimate company charges fees before settling a debt. If one asks, walk away.
  • Settlement is not the only path: compare it against consolidation and, for severe cases, bankruptcy counsel before enrolling.

FAQ

How much does debt relief cost? 15-25% of the debt you enroll, paid only as settlements complete. On $20,000 enrolled, expect $3,000-$5,000 in total fees.

How long does it take? Most programs run 24-48 months to full settlement.

Will debt relief hurt my credit? Yes, in the short term - missed payments during the program are reported. Most graduates rebuild within 12-24 months after completion.

Is debt relief the same as consolidation? No. Consolidation replaces your debts with one loan you repay in full. Settlement negotiates to pay less than you owe. They solve different problems.
