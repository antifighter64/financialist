California debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 4 years. Garnishment: Lesser of 20% of disposable pay or 40% above 48x minimum wage.

Texas debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 4 years. Garnishment: Not allowed (except child support/spousal maintenance).

Read the rules →Debt help

Florida debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 5 years (written); 4 years (oral). Garnishment: Head of family: fully protected up to $750/week.

Read the rules →Debt help

New York debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 3 years (consumer credit). Garnishment: Up to 10% of gross; nothing below 30x minimum wage.

Read the rules →Debt help

Pennsylvania debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 4 years. Garnishment: Not allowed for most consumer debts.

Read the rules →Debt help

Illinois debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 10 years (written); 5 years (oral). Garnishment: Lesser of 15% of gross pay or the amount above 45x minimum wage.

Read the rules →Debt help

Georgia debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 6 years (written); 4 years (open account). Garnishment: Lesser of 25% of disposable pay or the amount above $217.50/week.

Read the rules →Debt help

North Carolina debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 3 years. Garnishment: Earnings needed for your family are protected.

Read the rules →Debt help

Ohio debt collection laws

Sue deadline: 6 years (consumer debt). Garnishment: Protected: greater of 75% of disposable pay or 30x minimum wage/week.

New: money rights

Credit reports, wages, bank fees, student loans and medical bills, with verified state differences.

Credit reports: get your file and fix errors

The federal reporting rules apply in every state. Start with the report itself, then dispute a specific error with the bureau and the company that sent it.

Wages and overtime: check your hours

Federal overtime is a floor, not the complete answer. Your state, job duties and exemptions can change the calculation.

Bank fees: what an overdraft opt-in does

ATM and one-time debit-card overdraft fees have a specific federal opt-in rule. A check or recurring payment is not the same transaction.

Federal student loans: choose a repayment path

Check the loan type and current status in your Federal Student Aid account before relying on a payment estimate. Repayment programs and court orders can change.

Medical bills: check aid before paying

A medical bill can be wrong, eligible for financial assistance, or subject to collection rules. Ask for an itemized bill and your hospital’s assistance policy first.
