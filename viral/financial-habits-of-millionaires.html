Financial Habits of Millionaires

Forget the private jets and trust funds. Ramsey Solutions surveyed more than 10,000 American millionaires in its National Study of Millionaires — the largest study of its kind in North America — and the typical millionaire looks nothing like the stereotype.

By Financialist · October 17, 2026

Here are the habits and realities the data revealed, ranked from most surprising to most actionable.

1. They didn't inherit it — 79% got nothing

The single most myth-busting stat in the study: 79% of millionaires received zero inheritance from parents or other family members. While 21% got some inheritance, only 3% received $1 million or more. And 89% describe themselves as first-generation wealthy.

2. They weren't rich growing up — 80% came from middle income or below

Eight in ten millionaires grew up in families at or below middle-income level. Only 2% came from upper-income families. The starting line was ordinary.

3. They used the boring account — 8 in 10 invested in their 401(k)

No crypto windfalls, no angel-investing genius. Eight out of ten millionaires in the study built wealth through their employer's 401(k) plan — steady contributions, decades of compounding, employer match.

4. They didn't earn huge salaries — only 31% averaged $100K

Only 31% of the millionaires averaged $100,000 per year over their careers. One-third never made six figures in any single working year. They got rich on ordinary salaries by keeping more of what they earned.

5. They weren't bosses — only 15% were senior leaders

Just 15% ever held senior leadership roles like CEO or CFO. And 93% said they built wealth because they "worked hard" — not because they had big salaries or titles.

6. They worked in ordinary careers

The five careers that produced the most millionaires: engineers, accountants, management, attorneys, and teachers. Teachers. Nobody on that list got rich from a single deal — they got rich from decades of consistent saving.

7. They went to ordinary schools

Only 8% attended prestigious private schools. 62% graduated from public or state schools. Where you went to college barely registered as a factor.

8. They stayed out of debt

The study's consistent theme: millionaires avoid debt and watch expenses. No car loans on luxury vehicles, no lifestyle inflation. They built bank accounts instead of digging out of holes.

9. They started early and never stopped

The through-line of every finding: ordinary income, extraordinary consistency. Decades of automated 401(k) contributions did more than any stock pick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of millionaires inherited their money?

Per Ramsey Solutions' National Study of Millionaires, only 21% received any inheritance at all, and just 3% inherited $1 million or more.

Do you need a six-figure salary to become a millionaire?

No. Only 31% of the surveyed millionaires averaged $100,000 per year over their careers, and a third never earned six figures in any single working year.

What's the most common way millionaires built wealth?

Investing in their employer's 401(k) plan — 8 in 10 did it. Boring, automatic, compounded over decades.

What jobs do most millionaires have?

Engineers, accountants, managers, attorneys, and teachers top the list. Wealth came from consistency, not exotic careers.

Is it too late to start?

The math says starting at 40 with $500/month at a 10% average return still reaches $1 million around age 69. Late is better than never — by a lot.
