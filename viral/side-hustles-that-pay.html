Only 14% of Side Hustlers Crack $1,000/Month. Here's How They Do It.

Let's start with the unsexy truth, courtesy of Bankrate's 2025 survey: about 1 in 4 American adults has a side hustle. The average side hustler earns $885/month — but the median is just $200/month.

By Financialist · October 17, 2026

That gap tells you everything. A small number of hustlers earn serious money and drag the average up; most people earn pocket change. Only 25% earn more than $500/month, and just 14% clear $1,000/month.

So "side hustles that pay $1,000+/month" isn't a list of ideas — it's a list of ideas with a realistic path past the median. Every hustle below has people actually earning four figures monthly. Ranked from lowest effort to highest.

Tier 1: Low Effort, Steady Pay

1. Pet sitting / dog walking — ~$1,000–2,500/mo potential

Bankrate's survey puts pet care at 7% of all side hustles, and it's one of the few with genuinely low startup friction. Overnight sitting pays $40–75/night per pet in most metros; two regular weekend clients can clear $1,000/month. The constraint is your calendar, not your skills.

2. Food delivery (strategic hours only) — ~$800–1,500/mo potential

Food delivery is 9% of side hustles, but most drivers earn modestly because they work dead hours. The $1,000+ drivers work Friday–Sunday dinner rushes in dense zones and multi-app. Realistic math: 12–15 hrs/week at $20–25/hr during peaks ≈ $1,000–1,500/month. It's trading time for money at a known rate — no mystery, no upside.

Tier 2: Medium Effort, Real Upside

3. Freelance writing / editing — ~$1,500–4,000/mo potential

Professional and business services (writing, design, consulting) make up 14% of side hustles — the second-biggest category. Entry rates of $0.10–0.20/word mean a single 1,500-word article pays $150–300. Two articles a week = $1,200–2,400/month. The ramp is slow (portfolio → clients), but the ceiling is high and the work compounds.

4. Tutoring / test prep — ~$1,000–3,000/mo potential

Tutoring is 6% of side hustles. SAT/ACT and STEM tutors charge $50–100+/hour; 5 hours a week at $60/hr = $1,200/month. It's one of the highest hourly rates available without a professional license, and demand is famously recession-resistant (parents cut lattes before their kid's math tutor).

5. Online reselling — ~$1,000–3,000/mo potential

Online sales is the #1 side hustle category at 15%. The $1,000+ sellers aren't dropshipping — they're flipping underpriced goods (thrift stores, estate sales, clearance racks) on eBay/Mercari/Poshmark. Expect $300–800/month in the first 90 days while you learn what sells; experienced flippers clear $2,000+ with 10–15 hrs/week. Inventory risk is real — Bankrate's analyst specifically warns against buying inventory before you know what sells.

Tier 3: Higher Effort, Highest Ceiling

6. Bookkeeping for small businesses — ~$1,500–5,000/mo potential

Five clients at $300–500/month each = $1,500–2,500/month for roughly 20 hours of monthly work once systems are set. Small businesses desperately need this and hate doing it. A basic bookkeeping certification plus QuickBooks familiarity is enough to start; referrals do the marketing.

7. Handyman / home repair — ~$2,000–5,000/mo potential

Landscaping and home repair account for 4% of side hustles. Skilled handymen charge $60–100/hour with near-zero customer acquisition cost (Nextdoor + word of mouth). Weekend-only work at $75/hr × 8 hrs = $600/weekend. The constraint is skill and tools, not demand.

8. Building a digital product or audience — $0–10,000+/mo potential

The only hustle on this list with uncapped upside — and the only one where most people earn $0 for months. Templates, courses, newsletters, and niche content can scale past $1,000/month, but the failure rate is brutal. Treat it as a lottery ticket you work on after a Tier 1–2 hustle pays the bills.

The Three Rules of $1,000+ Hustles

  1. Sell to businesses or affluent individuals, not the mass market. The highest hourly rates come from clients with budgets (businesses, parents, homeowners).
  2. Pick something with recurring demand. One-off gigs keep you hunting; retainer clients (bookkeeping, regular pet sitting, weekly tutoring) compound.
  3. Count all your hours. The #1 reason hustles "pay well" on paper and poorly in reality: unpaid time (driving, listing, pitching, admin) never makes the Instagram post.

One more Bankrate stat worth sitting with: the average side hustler could pay off the average credit card balance ($6,371) in about seven months — meaningful, but only if the hustle income actually goes to the debt instead of lifestyle creep.

FAQ

Q: What percentage of people have a side hustle?

A: About 1 in 4 American adults (Bankrate, 2025). LendingTree's 2025 survey put it higher at 38% — survey definitions of "side hustle" vary, which explains the gap.

Q: How much does the average side hustle pay?

A: $885/month on average, but the median is just $200/month (Bankrate 2025). Only 14% of side hustlers earn more than $1,000/month.

Q: What is the most common side hustle?

A: Online sales (15%), followed by professional/business services like freelance writing and design (14%), then food delivery and crafts (9% each).

Q: Which side hustle pays the most per hour?

A: Skilled services: tutoring ($50–100+/hr), bookkeeping ($60–100+/hr at scale), and handyman work ($60–100/hr). These beat gig driving ($20–25/hr) by 2–4x per hour.

Q: Do I need to pay taxes on side hustle income?

A: Yes — it's taxable income, and you'll generally owe self-employment tax on net earnings above $400/year. Set aside 25–30% of profit as you go.
