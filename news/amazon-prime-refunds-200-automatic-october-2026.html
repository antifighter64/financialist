Amazon Prime Refunds Expand to $200; More Checks Start October 1

A revised FTC-Amazon order raises the Prime refund cap to $200 and adds automatic payments starting October 1, 2026. Who may be included and how to avoid refund scams.

By David Chen · September 27, 2026 at 7:08 AM EDT

More former and current Prime customers may receive refunds without filing a new claim beginning October 1 under a revised federal court order. The FTC says the maximum total payment rises from $51 to $200 and Amazon must distribute future payments automatically. A $200 cap is not a promise of a $200 check to every subscriber.

The revision expands automatic payments to people who used between 11 and 20 Prime benefits in a one-year period after enrollment; those people were outside the earlier phases. Amazon will make payments electronically through Venmo or PayPal or mail checks, the FTC says. Customers should not need to respond to a new claim notice or complete additional forms to receive these future payments.

There may also be a later top-up for people who already received and accepted a settlement refund. If accepted payments do not reach the required threshold by February 2027, the FTC says those consumers could get up to $149 more, bringing their total up to $200; that supplemental round would start by April 2027. The condition matters: it is not an unconditional additional payment today.

The FTC says it is not contacting consumers about these Amazon refunds and warns that anyone claiming to be the agency and asking for payment is likely a scam. Amazon will not ask customers to pay a fee to receive a refund. Check the September 17 FTC release for the revised terms. Its separate refund-program FAQ still described an older $51 cap when checked September 27, so the newer court-order announcement controls this update; readers should watch for that older FAQ to be revised.
